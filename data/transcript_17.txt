Transcript 17 — Intro Call
Prospect: Patrick D.
Industry: Sales Training / Coaching
Date: 2024-10-07

Sean: Patrick, what's going on?

Patrick: I run sales training for B2B companies — mostly helping mid-market sales teams improve pipeline generation and discovery call skills. I've been independent for five years. Good business but I'm at a scale problem.

Sean: What's the scale problem?

Patrick: I want to go from $250K to $500K in the next 18 months. But every dollar I make right now requires me personally to do the work. I want to build a model where some of the delivery can be leveraged — either through better systems or eventually through other facilitators. But I don't have the infrastructure for either.

Sean: What would need to be true to leverage the delivery?

Patrick: My training curriculum would need to be documented in a way that someone else could deliver it. My client onboarding would need to not require me. My sales process for new clients — which is all relationship-based and lives in my head — would need to be written down somewhere.

Sean: When you say the sales process lives in your head — what does that mean practically?

Patrick: I know who my best prospects are. I know what questions to ask. I know how to move from a conversation to a proposal. But none of that is written down. If I had a business partner or an SDR, I couldn't hand them anything useful today.

Sean: That's the core constraint. The business is a Patrick-shaped system. To scale it, you have to extract Patrick from the architecture and replace him with documented process.

Patrick: That's exactly right. And I know it. I've just been too busy doing the work to build the infrastructure.

Sean: That's the catch-22 of every founder I work with. The answer is usually a short, focused engagement — not a year-long transformation. One process at a time.

Patrick: Where would you start with something like mine?

Sean: The sales process documentation. That's your revenue engine. Everything downstream gets easier once that's transferable.
